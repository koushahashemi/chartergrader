General Information
How does Greenify earn revenue?

Response
Greenify is a combination hardware/software company with approximately 10 years of experience. They specialize in creating networked sensors to collect data which can then be analysed to provide insights across various areas.
In the past Greenify has deployed sensors in several major cities to monitor air quality and provided maps to city planners to identify areas of concern.
City of Vancouver will be Greenify’s first client for their new sensor network which measures data around waste management, particularly garbage bin usage and status.
Previous projects have allowed Greenify to become experts in areas such as:
Construction of highly rugged sensors capable of withstanding adverse weather conditions for up to 5 years.
Secure wifi based sharing of data between sensors and centralized databases for collection of data.
Analysis of environmental data in a geographic context to provide meaningful insights.
Greenify won the contract with CoV by bidding on a Request for Proposal (RFP) from the city. The contract starts January 1, 2027 and runs for 5 years, at which point a mutual option to renew may be exercised by both CoV and Greenify. The contract is fixed price.
We earn revenue from hardware sales, sensor deployments, data analytics services, and ongoing support contracts. 
We became aware of the potential challenges while preparing our proposal for the City. Once we started reviewing the City's existing system, we realized that more work would be needed to integrate the two platforms.

Response
Neither CoV nor Greenify have comprehensive ERDs or documentation of their data models. Our best bet is probably to review sample data from CoV and the Greenify databases to map the data of each (to themselves and to each other). After the project charter is completed and signed off we can provide sample data from CoV and grant read access to the Greenify databases.
We do have a sample ERD that highlights some of the challenges of integrating the data.
Some of the challenges arise from different data formats. For example, CoV enters dates on which each bin is emptied based on routes driven that day. Greenify's data model includes a field for seconds since each bin was emptied, but we will need the City's collection records to populate this field once the systems are integrated. CoV keeps track of the street address of each bin, while Greenify tracks the coordinates (latitude and longitude) of each sensor.
In general, Greenify’s data model is built around its sensors, while CoV keeps track of bins, routes, drivers, etc.
One critical aspect of integration: Greenify’s trackers are placed at the mouth of each bin and measures the number of items placed in a bin to estimate fullness. They do not track when a bin is emptied and will rely on CoV data for this information. For this reason, we do need to maintain the CoV system and integrate the data between each system.
Greenify receives data from its sensors every 15 minutes. CoV updates its data every day. We will need to synchronize data at least daily, but preferably in the morning to account for planned routes that day and in the evening to account for any disruptions to the planned routes.
We check for unusual readings and missing data. If a sensor stops reporting or provides questionable information, we'll flag it for review. For route planning, we may need to rely on the last reliable reading or the City's collection records.
Greenify is planning to build a tool which imports CSV files which are exported by CoV databases. The tool is out of scope, but having a reliable map of the data for both systems and between systems will expedite the building of the tool.
CoV Keeps their data on a cloud service and Greenify has their own data centre. Both system back up data weekly.
Greenify currently uses an algorithm to calculate bin fullness and urgency, but they don't use AI for this. AI may be something we explore in the future, but it's outside this project's scope. You can use AI tools with sample data, but confidential Greenify or City data must not be shared without approval.
We would like at least 98% of bin records to be matched correctly. Any unmatched records should be documented and investigated. This is a proposed planning target, not an existing contractual requirement.
The two systems have bin IDs, but we cannot assume they match. We'll need to create a mapping between them. The City will remain the source of truth for bin locations and collection records, while Greenify will be responsible for sensor readings.
Greenify has reviewed the basic data structures and identified some differences between the two systems. We haven't built the integration tool yet.

Response
Greenify puts a high value on security and has emphasized the importance of securing the data pipelines between their sensors and their centralized systems. For this project, assume this business process is out of scope.
However, the City of Vancouver as a governmental institution needs to ensure that Greenify is following best practices with all of its organizational data and is in compliance with Canada’s Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)
At the outset of Greenify’s contract with City of Vancouver they must have a documented Data Governance Framework which demonstrates that they have policies, processes and procedures in place to properly govern all organizational data.
Greenify would like to leverage existing industry examples of Data Governance Frameworks to avoid reinventing the wheel. Comparing and choosing an appropriate framework would help to provide Greenify with a roadmap towards implementing these policies, processes and procedures and satisfying CoV.
The sensors don't collect personal information, but some City records contain employee and operational details. Only authorized staff should have access. We don't currently share data with third parties. If we select a route-planning vendor, we'll review its security practices and limit access to the data it needs.
Greenify uses encrypted connections to transfer sensor data to our systems. Security of the sensor communication network is already handled internally and is outside this project's scope.
Greenify hasn't experienced any major security incidents that need to be addressed in this project.
In case of an unlikely security incident, report it immediately to Kousha. Do not try to investigate or resolve the incident independently. Our technical team will take responsibility for handling it.


Software Selection for Route Optimization
Functional Requirements
What are the most important features and functionalities required in the route optimization software?

Preferred Options
Has Greenify researched or considered specific software solutions?
Are there existing vendor relationships or technology preferences?
Does Greenify prefer a single recommendation or a ranked shortlist?
Response
Greenify is able to use its sensors to map out high priority areas of the city. You can see an example of an internal mapping tool in “Heat Map.png”. 
Clusters of bins are assigned a size based on their level of fullness and an urgency based on an internal algorithm which factors in the type of material (compost, recycling, waste), as well as environmental factors such as temperature and humidity. For example, compost in a high temperature and humidity area will smell worse and increase the urgency score for a bin.
Each day, Greenify would like to create a single route which will hit the top 30 map clusters in terms of fullness and urgency. This route will then be divided amongst the available drivers and vehicles that day.
Greenify has the capability of generating the list of 30 points, but will require a tool which can create the most efficient route between them.
This will be done each morning approximately 2hours before drivers arrive to start their routes. It will not be updated throughout the day to reflect real-time conditions (this would be a possible future enhancement).
Key features include:
Ability to prioritize at least 30 stops in a single route
Ability to generate at least 1 route every business day of the year
Ability to upload a single file (or connect to a single) API with all stop information to minimize manual labour
At this point Greenify does not know what is available in the market and does not know their budget. They will require a market survey to assess the tools available, features and cost/benefit of each tool.
Students are not expected to code a route engine, install sensors or implement production software.
A shortlist of two or three suitable options, with a clear recommendation, would be helpful. We want to understand the advantages, disadvantages, and costs before making a final decision.

You can use publicly available information to evaluate software options. If you feel a vendor demonstration or quotation is necessary, discuss it with Kousha first. We don't expect you to negotiate contracts or purchase software.
